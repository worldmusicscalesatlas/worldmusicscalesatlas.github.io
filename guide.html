Atlas guide

Find a tradition

Drag the globe with one finger or a mouse. Pinch or use the zoom buttons to look closer. Select a marker, a label, or an area to see its scales. Browse regions offers the same choices by name. Some markers are labelled context only or research pending. They link to public cultural resources when available and contain no playable tuning. Map anchors are approximate navigation aids, not cultural boundaries.

Read the wheel

Start at the top and read clockwise. Each colored arc spans the distance between successive pitches. Its width shows the interval in cents. One octave is 1,200 cents. Models with an open span use a strip to show their measured range.

Change the tonic

Drag the inner circle, tap its left half to lower the tonic by 50 cents, or tap its right half to raise it by 50 cents. With the wheel focused, use the left and right arrow keys. Transposition preserves each interval. Play scale lets you hear the result.

Explore notes and sources

Signature shows note spellings, alterations and exact pitch positions. Info contains the model description and references. Choose Compare beside Play scale to select or remove a comparison. The diagram aligns both models at the same tonic. Read the primary scale on the outer ring and the comparison on the inner ring. Tap an interval for its distance from the tonic and the nearest pitch in the other scale. Arrows beside pitch names mean above or below the nearest equal-tempered note. Open spans use a shared linear cent axis. Select the small globe to return to your region.
