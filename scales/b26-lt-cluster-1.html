Evidence and interpretation

Primary numerical evidence rechecked

Table 4 original text explicitly labels adjacent I–II, II–III, III–IV and IV–V means. Thesis summary confirms methodology. No source facsimile inspection possible.

Mean interval structure for cluster 1 in the authors' ten-cluster analysis of 51 selected scales from their 73-song monophonic sample. The four adjacent intervals of degrees I–V are converted from reported semitones to cents; no normalization or octave closure. The cluster label equitonic-like reflects the authors' interpretation. This is a statistical mean, not an individual performance, not a complete five-tone octave mode, and not a universal Lithuanian scale. The table reports six selected clusters; per-cluster sample counts and uncertainty are not inferred.

Publicly reported pitch facts or statistical aggregates only. No recordings, song transcriptions, diagrams or ceremonial material reproduced. No claim of wider community endorsement.

A numerical pitch pattern is not a complete account of a musical tradition, performance practice or melodic function. Equal interval patterns do not establish cultural equivalence.
