Evidence and interpretation

Secondary source theoretical structure checked

Five source-reported theoretical pitch patterns independently encoded as semitone distances. The 1983 original and rendered PDF page images were unavailable; no acoustic validation claimed.

One of five theoretical anhemitonic pentatonic octave species discussed by Luvsangenden (2023), pp. 21–22, citing Jantsannorov (1983). Numbering here is an Atlas identifier, not a traditional modal name. The semitone encoding does not measure historical or contemporary performance intonation. Mongolian long songs may shift between linked pitch collections, and ornamentation can include microtonal inflections. The first pitch is an analytical/playback reference, not a unique modal tonic.

Publicly documented abstract pitch patterns only; no source figures, recordings, songs or ceremonial materials reproduced. No community endorsement asserted.

A numerical pitch pattern is not a complete account of a musical tradition, performance practice or melodic function. Equal interval patterns do not establish cultural equivalence.
